# WP19 — structured resource-limit errors and canonical telemetry

Status: implementation and local forced-native checkpoint complete; pinned incident telemetry pending
Prepared: 2026-08-02

## Outcome

Configured resource-limit failures now cross the Python/native boundary as a
typed contract. `ResourceLimitError` requires non-empty `limit` plus finite,
non-negative `observed` and `allowed` values. Its bounded `details` mapping is
immutable and is preserved by `as_diagnostic()`.

Rust carries the same values in `NativeResourceLimit`; the PyO3 boundary
accepts only the exact bounded payload shape. A missing, malformed, or
message-derived payload fails closed as `NATIVE_LIMIT_PAYLOAD`. Panic,
protocol, cancellation, and injected allocator failures retain their separate
categories.

Anonymous canonicalization exposes only bounded numeric telemetry. The stable
limit-detail vocabulary is `component_count`, `largest_component_labels`,
`largest_component_arcs`, `refinement_rounds`, and `work_term`. Success
telemetry additionally records aggregate and maximum component/work counters.
Blank labels, source excerpts, and ontology-sized collections are not exposed.

The configured-threshold census was completed across native limit, parser,
wire, index, publication, and closure paths. Configured comparisons use typed
resource-limit constructors. Remaining unstructured `NativeError::limit`
sites represent arithmetic conversion/overflow, allocation failure, injected
failure, or other non-configured internal failure classes.
